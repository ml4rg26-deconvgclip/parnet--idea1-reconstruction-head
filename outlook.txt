1. THE CORE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The reconstruction loss only ever sees the AGGREGATE globalCLIP signal,
never the contributions of individual proteins. This creates a real
identifiability problem: if two proteins (e.g. PTBP1, PTBP2, PUF60) have
strongly correlated, similar Parnet tracks, the model can shift weight
between them arbitrarily without the reconstructed signal changing
measurably. The training loss is blind to this ambiguity — it cannot
distinguish "correct" attribution from "numerically equivalent but wrong"
attribution.

Partial solution already in place: the log-amplitude s_k is fixed per
protein across ALL windows/positions (not relearned per window) — this
prevents window-level overfitting, but does NOT solve the risk of confusing
different, but correlated, proteins with each other. This is the actual
reason QLayer was originally developed: the phase-interference mechanism
was meant to give correlated proteins a distinguishable "fingerprint" via
the cross-terms with all 222 other proteins — structure a plain linear sum
cannot represent.

QLayer/CombiLayer have not demonstrably solved this so far, though:
statistically tied with CNN-only on accuracy (within 0.007 Pearson), and
the coupling-matrix analysis is still inconclusive (only cooperative, no
competitive couplings found; expected validation proteins such as
spliceosome components U2AF1/U2AF2, SF3B4, PRPF8 are missing from the
dominant proteins) — and it has so far only been run on an earlier model
version, not on the final filtered checkpoints.


2. NEW IDEA FROM THE CALL: EMPIRICAL CORRELATION AS AN INDEPENDENT CHECK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Proposal: instead of just hoping QLayer's learned coupling matrix reflects
real protein-protein cooperation, check it INDEPENDENTLY — directly from
the raw, uncombined Parnet tracks, with no training loss or mixing weights
involved at all:

  For every protein pair (i, j): compute the correlation between
  track_i(p) and track_j(p) across all positions p, and across many
  training windows.

This "raw" correlation is not ambiguous — it's based only on Parnet's own
independent per-protein predictions, before any combination happens. High
correlation = the two proteins consistently predict high values at the same
positions = genuine evidence of co-occurrence (cooperation), or of
confusability risk.

TWO POSSIBLE WAYS TO USE THIS:

  (a) Validation / diagnostic (recommended as the FIRST step):
      Compute the raw track-correlation matrix (no retraining needed, just
      a computation on existing checkpoints/data) and compare it against
      QLayer's learned coupling matrix (cos(phase_i - phase_j)). If they
      agree → strong, independent evidence that QLayer's phase mechanism
      learned genuine structure, not noise. If they DON'T agree → likely
      explains why the interaction analysis has been inconclusive so far
      (see section 1).

  (b) Explicit loss term (only if (a) shows a discrepancy):
      Add the raw correlation matrix as an additional training target that
      pulls the learned coupling matrix toward the empirical co-activity —
      giving the model real supervision on interaction structure, instead
      of hoping it falls out of the pure reconstruction loss. Cost: a new
      hyperparameter (weighting against the Pearson/NLL/sparsity terms
      already present), requires retraining, adds complexity.

RECOMMENDATION: do (a) first, only do (b) if actually needed.
  - (a) is essentially free: no retraining, doable immediately with
    existing checkpoints/data, gives an answer this week.
  - (a) is diagnostic BEFORE it's corrective: right now we don't know
    whether the coupling matrix reflects real signal or noise — this check
    answers that directly, before investing effort in a new loss term.
  - Only if (a) shows the learned coupling matrix does NOT match the raw
    correlation is (b) worth the effort — at that point we'd know the pure
    reconstruction loss genuinely isn't enough and explicit supervision is
    needed.


3. FURTHER OPEN ITEMS (from earlier discussions, still valid)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Re-run the QLayer/CombiLayer coupling-matrix analysis on the final,
  outlier-filtered checkpoints (current results are from an earlier model
  version and not yet final).
- Stability selection / ensemble: compare multiple independent training
  runs (different seeds or bootstrap resamples) and only trust proteins
  whose high weighting is consistent across runs — addresses the observed
  instability (global vs. positional mixing give completely different
  top-5 proteins).
- Test control-track normalization empirically after all (Lysate_noNHS
  etc.) — could strip out shared background and sharpen protein-specific
  signal, even though its suitability hasn't been officially confirmed.
- Biologically informed grouping: use known protein complexes (spliceosome,
  EJC, hnRNP family, from STRING-DB or literature) as a structural prior to
  reduce the degrees of freedom in the 223 weights and make attribution
  more structurally identifiable, instead of learning everything freely.
- Additionally validate the coupling matrix against independent
  protein-interaction databases (STRING-DB), not just motif databases.
- Normalize the motif-detection significance threshold onto a comparable
  scale between the 7M and 21M backbones, to check whether the stronger
  fragmentation seen with the 21M backbone is a real effect or just an
  artifact of different IG attribution scales.
- Generate consensus-motif letter-code plots for the refined Idea-1 models
  (needs the server: RBP motif database + metamotif tool not available
  locally).


4. KEY TAKEAWAY FOR THE OUTLOOK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Best predictive accuracy (highest Pearson r) and best protein-level
interpretability are two separate goals that, in this project so far, move
in opposite directions (21M backbone: best predictor, weakest motif/
interaction resolution). The right next step is not to throw more capacity
or architectural complexity at the accuracy objective, but to deliberately
add information the pure reconstruction loss is missing — first and
foremost, the raw, uncombined protein correlation as a signal for genuine
cooperation between proteins.
